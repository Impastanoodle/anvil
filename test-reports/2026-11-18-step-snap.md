## 1. A Miter Flange lip folded less than 90 deg off the riser: its bends whole in STEP

Part: 4 x 2 base, 0.06 sheet, R .06, K .5, Miter Flange round the corner on an L profile, riser 1, lip .5 at `deg`
above level (0 = the plain L, the lip square to the riser).

**What was wrong.** The lip flares out, and the two lips' bends cross at the mitre: their outer (convex) cylinders
overlap and meet in an ellipse in the bisector plane, and the seam ends where the two bends' ends (fans of planar
chords, a non-planar quad per zone split by a diagonal) meet. At that point four surfaces meet (both cylinders, one end
triangle of each bend) and they have no common point: the two sides' quads are split by different diagonals, so the
point on both cylinders and one end plane is 2.1e-5 off the other end plane. The mesh had the vertex where the four
facets meet, 2.2e-4 inside both cylinders. `snapToCylinders` moves a vertex off one cylinder only, `snapCylEqual` a
vertex on two cylinders and nothing else, so both lip bends' cylinders went to the file as facets and Insert Bends
found 2 of 4 bends (blank 20 % short). The other seam vertices were already handled (`snapCylEqual`).

**Fix (exchange.js `splitCrossings`, called by `writeBrep`).** A vertex whose fan is cylinder, plane, plane, cylinder
and that lies off a cylinder becomes two vertices joined by a short edge, as a true B-rep has it: the line where the two
planes meet stops at the cylinder it reaches first from that edge's far end (line and cylinder), the other vertex is
the plane between on both cylinders (Newton). The edge between them is a plane's cut of a cylinder (an ELLIPSE run).
Done on a copy of the mesh (two triangles added); when a patch at a split vertex falls back to facets anyway the body
is written again without the split (the writer's text is rolled back). `Exchange.crossOptions.on = false`: as before.

**Not reached: blank = own flat to 1e-9 for a flared lip (3e-3 to 4.5e-3 in^2 apart).** This is not the file: the
folded part itself has the two lips' bends overlapping (the read-back volume equals the part's), and the two flats
draw the lip bend's end by different rules. Measured on 60 deg (own flat, the riser's end at x = .06707, lip at

## 2. Hex nut and Moulded cap: cones whole in STEP

**What was wrong.** A cone cut by planes (the nut's 45 deg chamfer cones by its six flats: hyperbolas; the cap's 2 deg
drafted boss by its 1 deg drafted rib) had (a) its corner vertices on the faceted cone's chords (snapToCylinders is
cylinders only) and (b) no edge class for a plane's cut of a cone (ellipseEdge is cylinders only), so the cones went
out as facets.

**Fix (exchange.js).** `snapToCones`: a vertex off one cone, fixed by two planes (the top face and a flat; two flats)
or by one plane square to the axis, moves along that line onto the cone (undone with the patch like the cylinders'
snaps). `conicEdge`: a cone edge whose neighbour is a true plane, ends on the plane, on the cone or inside by at most a
sagitta, is class `k` (key: cone + plane); a run of them is one EDGE_CURVE, `conicCurve`: rational quadratic B-spline
pieces (at most 60 deg of turn each), the middle pole where the conic's end tangents meet, weight lam / (1 - lam) from
where the line from the chord's middle to that pole crosses the cone: exact on the conic (hyperbola, ellipse or
parabola). A snapped cone patch of shape `plain` is written (Anvil's reader takes it by `surfPatch`). A loop that is
one whole conic still keeps its facets.

## 3. Fuzz round 464: why the first attempt fails

Round 464 (rfFT round fuzz): plate, a Ø.5 and a Ø.75 boss, three holes through, `fillet(.25)` at the Ø.75 boss's
root, then R.225 on a hole's rim, then a chamfer. The R.25 round's guard went s2, s1, s4, band .5, band 2, heal,
plain: 68, 3, 13, 60, 35, 33, 58 open edges, then the plain round (0).

Measured: the round's tool (`arcChainTool`, rails where the ball rests on the Ø.5 hole's rim) is closed on every
attempt (0 open / 0 non-manifold). The cracks come from taking the inner loop's prism (the boss's footprint merged with
the hole, `rfGGholePrisms`) off the tool: every open edge lies on the hole's faceted wall (0.2488 to 0.2499 from its
axis) at 117 to 120 deg and 165 to 167 deg round the boss: where the hole's wall is tangent to the boss's radial lines
(117.1 and 167.1 deg) and where the setback leaves the plate for the hole (118.7, 165.6 deg). Two things meet there:
(a) the last rail ring (its setback a third of a station inside the hole, ball resting on the rim) and the first
plain ring are joined by triangles twisted across the hole's wall; (b) the hole's wall cuts the tool almost along its
rings (its facets nearly in the boss's meridian planes).

Tried for (a), not kept (kernel-mesh.js unchanged): a station at the crossing itself in `rfGGstations` (bisected to
where the setback leaves the plane, on the rail's side, where the rail ring is nearly the plain one). Round 464 then
closes on its 3rd attempt (42 / 31 / 0 open: s4, the rail round, instead of the plain round on the 8th), but the
first attempt still cracks (b), and over round fuzz 1..1000 it moved 19 of the 31 rows with rails (2e-9 to 1.3e-4,
seed 69 the most, four rows slower) with no reference to say which way is better; all 27 part examples were
bit-identical. The rail ring is not the plain one even at the crossing (its wall point is pushed 1e-3 size into the
air, its inner points sit at `rIn`), so a station there still leaves a step. What would fix (b): keep the rings off
the hole's tangent meridians, or take the hole's prism off with the rail strips' triangles split where the wall
crosses them. Left for a fillet lane, with the probe: `.scratch`-style runner over rfFT's fuzz table,
`K.openGuard.toolLog` (the tool is closed) and `K.openGuard.log` (the result after the prism is not).
